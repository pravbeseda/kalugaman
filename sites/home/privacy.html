Privacy policy

Effective September 27, 2026

This policy covers the Home Chrome extension and its companion native host (together, “Home”), made by Alexander Ivanov (“I”).

The short version

I collect nothing. Home has no account, no server of its own, no analytics and no telemetry. Your data stays in your browser and on your computer, and nothing is sold or shared with anyone.

Data stored in your browser

Home keeps its settings and cached widget data in Chrome's local extension storage (chrome.storage.local) on your device: the bookmark folder you picked, the widget layout, whether notifications are on, the latest widget readings, and — if you set up the monitor widget — the hub address, login and password you entered. This data is not synced to other devices and is deleted when you remove the extension.

Data read on your computer

The optional native host runs locally on your computer and answers only this extension. It reads the free space of your disks and the usage-limit snapshots that Claude Code and Codex leave on disk. These readings are shown on the page and never leave your computer.

Network requests

Home talks to only two services, both chosen and configured by you:

GitHub
If the native host is installed, it periodically asks the GitHub API for your pull requests, using the token of the account you are signed in with in the GitHub CLI (gh). The request goes straight from your computer to GitHub; the token is not stored by Home. GitHub's own privacy statement applies to that request.
Your monitoring hub
If you enter a hub address, the extension periodically requests its status from that address with the login and password you entered, over HTTPS only. Chrome asks for your permission to access that one address first.

Site icons are taken from Chrome's own local favicon cache, not downloaded by Home.

Why Home needs each permission

Bookmarks
To show the folder you picked and let you rearrange, rename and add bookmarks.
Sessions, top sites
To list recently closed tabs and your most visited sites.
Tabs
To open a link or a notification in a tab.
Favicon
To show site icons from Chrome's local cache.
Storage, alarms
To keep settings locally and refresh widgets on a schedule.
Notifications
To tell you about changes in your pull requests. You can turn them off.
Native messaging
To talk to the companion host on your computer.
Access to a website (optional)
Requested only for the monitoring hub address you enter, and only then.
